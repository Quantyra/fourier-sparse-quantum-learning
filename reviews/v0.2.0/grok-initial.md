**Verdict: GO WITH NOTES.** The sample bound, the \(2/3\) success claim, archive safety for arbitrary proposals, and the every-tape polynomial caps follow from the arguments as written. No blocking gap showed up in the changed mechanism or the retained lemmas. Notes are missing one-line justifications and scope limits, not counter-examples.

## What the revision is doing

Proposal work (residual estimates, flags, majority, prefix search) is allowed to fail with constant probability \(\rho=1/16\). Archive updates use a fresh batch and a much smaller failure budget \(\beta=1/(200T)\). Progress is counted by indicators that are forced to \(1\) after a safety failure or after \(W=0\), without conditioning the whole run on future safety. That is a legitimate way to replace a \(\log^3\) repetition with \(T=\Theta(\log(2k))\) constant-confidence trials.

## Checks that hold

**Arbitrary proposals.** On a history with \(U\subseteq S\) and the fit invariant, any list \(L\) with \(|L|\le P\) is fixed before the certification batch. Lemma (certificate) plus the estimate event \(C\) admits only indices in \(S\). The Gram event \(G\) is one matrix-Chernoff event on \(S\cup\{0\}\), so every principal submatrix and cross-block is controlled with no union over subsets. The algebraic identity \(a_U-c_U=(A_U^\top A_U/q)^{-1}(A_U^\top A_V/q)c_V\) then gives \(\lVert a-P_Uf\rVert_2\le\sqrt{W}/9\) for the data-dependent \(U^+\subseteq S\). False lists are rejected; \(W\) does not increase; a singular system or \(\lVert a\rVert_2^2>2\) aborts. Dependence between \(C\) and \(G\) is absorbed by a \(2\beta\) union bound. This is the right shared-batch argument.

The norm comparison asserted in the fit lemma is true and sharp. With \(t=\sqrt{W}\), \(\sqrt{1-t^2}+t/9\) has maximum \(\sqrt{82}/9=\sqrt{82/81}\) at \(t=1/\sqrt{82}\). So \(\lVert a\rVert_2^2\le 82/81<2\), and the abort threshold does not fire on \(G\).

**Contraction arithmetic.** On the estimator event, cutoff \(t=4\varepsilon\sqrt{v/k}\) satisfies \(2\sqrt{2}\,\Delta\le t\le 2\sqrt{6}\,\Delta<5\Delta\). Passing weights obey \(|c_s/\widetilde c_s|<3/2\); failing coefficients obey \(|c_s|<6\Delta\). Hence \(\lVert h-f\rVert_2^2\le 36\varepsilon^2 e^2\) and a single weighted draw has second moment \(\le 9k/4\). With \(m=144k\), Chebyshev gives conditional sign error \(\le 1/16\) off a set of measure \(\le 4\lVert h-f\rVert_2^2\). For odd \(g\), \(2^g(1/16)^{g/2}=2^{-g}\). The parameter match \(\varepsilon^2=\rho/262144\) and \(2^g\ge 8192k^2\) is exact, and \(W\ge 1/k^2\) puts both Markov terms at most \(\beta W/128\).

Search omission gives \(\sum_{S\setminus L}c_s^2\le(16/9)\lVert H-f\rVert_2^2\le W/36\). Certification misses contribute at most \(16\gamma^2 e^2\le\alpha^2 W/64\). Then
\[
\frac1{36}+\frac{82}{81\cdot 64}=\frac{226}{5184}<\frac{324}{5184}=\frac1{16}.
\]
So \(W(U^+)<W/16\) on the good proposal events intersected with \(C\cap G\).

A flat union bound on unconditional majority failure would be slightly larger than \(4\rho\), because that failure is controlled only on the estimator event. Read as a staged bound it is valid: \(\Pr(A_1)\), \(\Pr(A_2\mid A_1)\), \(\Pr(A_3\mid A_1,A_2)\), and \(\Pr(\mathrm{search}\mid H)\) are each at least \(1-\rho\), and flags preserve the conditional law \(c_s^2\). Thus the joint proposal probability is at least \((15/16)^4>3/4\). Intersecting an independent fresh batch yields at least \((3/4)(1-2\beta)\ge 3/4-2\beta\ge 1/2\). Even a crude \(1-5\rho-2\beta\) stays above \(1/2\). The \(1/2\) fed to the supermartingale is safe. Also, \(T=8\ell\) is the smallest integer multiple that works: \(2(3/4)^7>1/4\) while \(2(3/4)^8=6561/32768<1/4\).

**Indicators.** \(\mathbb E[2^{-I_t}\mid\mathcal F_{t-1}]\le 3/4\) on every history, including dummy histories, so \(\mathbb E[2^{-Z_T}]\le(3/4)^T\) without independent trials. Markov gives \(\Pr(Z_T<\ell)<1/4\). Safety failures satisfy \(\Pr(B)\le 2T\beta=1/100\) by a first-failure decomposition, not by conditioning on \(B^c\). On \(B^c\), dummy “already failed” indicators never turn on; dummy “\(W=0\)” indicators turn on only after absorption; every other \(1\) is a factor-\(16\) contraction and every \(0\) is nonincreasing. So \(Z_T\ge\ell\) forces
\[
W<16^{-\ell}\le\frac1{16k^4}<\frac1{k^2}.
\]
The \(16^{-\ell}\) bound is tight when \(2k\) is a power of two; strictness comes from \(1/(16k^4)<1/k^2\). Granularity then forces \(W=0\), the last good refit equals \(f\), and the output checks pass on that event. Overlap only helps: success probability is greater than \(37/50>2/3\). Dummies after a real safety failure are not treated as correctness.

**Granularity.** For sparsity \(1\), \(f\) is a signed parity and \(|c|=1\). For sparsity \(s_0>1\), the cited form (integer multiples of \(2^{1-\lfloor\log_2 s_0\rfloor}\)) does imply magnitude at least \(2/s_0\ge 1/k\). A positive missing mass is then at least \(1/k^2\). This step stands or falls with that citation; the arithmetic from the stated form is correct.

**Every tape, including failures.** Copy use \(T(q_e+4gm+q_c)+\lceil 2k\ln 100\rceil\) is deterministic and \(O(k\log^2(2k))\): \(\rho,\varepsilon,\gamma\) are absolute constants, \(g,T=\Theta(\log(2k))\), and \(\ln(P/\beta)=O(\log(2k))\). Prefix-search \(\ln n\) hits evaluations of \(H\) only. Replacing \(\ln\) by \(\lceil\log_2\rceil\) only increases counts. Empty lists, flag shortages, search \(\mathsf{FAIL}\), the distinct-count guard, singular systems, and \(|U|>k\) are capped; overflow is checked before a fit, so the Gram is at most \(k\times k\), not \(P\times P\). Momentary storage of at most \(k+P\) indices is \(O(nk^2)\) bits and fits the space bound. Hadamard bit length \(b_a=O(k\log(2kq_c))\) holds for every nonsingular integer Gram with entries at most \(q_c\), wrong archive included. Inverse-weight denominators have bit length \(\widetilde O(k^2)\) because numerators are integers, including the \(v=0\) tape where every nonzero estimate passes the cutoff. Schoolbook evaluation is \(\widetilde O(nk+k^3)\) per query; \(O(nk^2)\) tests times \(O(k^4\log(nk))\) samples times \(T\) rounds reproduce \(\widetilde O(n^2k^7+nk^9)\) and \(\widetilde O(nk^2+k^3)\). Zero inverses, \(\operatorname{sgn}(0)=1\), and exact \(a^2=1\) by a \(k^2\)-term convolution are specified. Certification uses a strict inequality, so a zero residual admits nothing.

**Endpoints.** For \(k=1\), \(\ell=1\), \(T=8\), one contraction sends \(W<1/16<1\). Ideal flag-one samples lie in \(S\), so the distinct-count guard has probability \(0\). If \(W=0\), residuals are identically zero, no new index is admitted, and a good refit stays \(f\). A later singular Gram can still abort an already exact archive; that tape is inside \(B\) and is allowed to return \(\mathsf{FAIL}\). A wrong \(H\) only changes \(L\); on \(C\) the archive is unchanged. False accept of a different \(k\)-sparse Boolean function has probability at most \(1/100\), because disagreement is at least \(1/(2k)\) and validation is fresh. That bound is not used in the \(37/50\) estimate. The initial state \(U=\varnothing\), \(a=0\), \(W=1\) meets the invariant with no samples. Amplitude of outcome \((s,1)\) is \(c_s/\sqrt{2}\), so the flag has probability \(1/2\) and the conditional index law is \(c_s^2\).

Bernstein and Hoeffding constants were re-derived from the stated forms. Estimate tails fit in \(2(P+1)e^{-L_0}=\beta/2\). Prefix tests: range \([-1,1]\), deviation \(\tau^2/4\), \(N\) as written, at most \(2nP\) tests, union \(\le\beta\). Retained buckets have energy at least \(\tau^2/2\), so at most \(2/\tau^2\le P=64k^2\) survive. Matrix-Chernoff exponents \(\delta^2/2\) and \(\delta^2/3\) at \(\delta=1/10\) give the factor \(300\) with equality in the exponent budget; ceilings only help.

## Notes, not defects

The fit-norm maximum is not shown; it is one calculus line and it is sharp. The inequality \(\exp(-gm/4)\le\rho\) is true for \(g\ge 13\) and \(m=144k\) but not written numerically. The four-event \(1-4\rho\) claim should say “staged,” so a reader does not apply a flat union to the unconditional majority event. The algorithm never accepts early: after \(W=0\), a later Gram failure still returns \(\mathsf{FAIL}\) and discards an exact fit. That probability is already charged to \(\beta\). Leading constants are far from optimized (\(\varepsilon=1/2048\) makes \(q_e\) enormous at \(k=1\)); the manuscript already says the bounds are asymptotic and not a practical runtime. Integer Markov can replace \(2^\ell\) by \(2^{\ell-1}\); the published \(8\) still clears \(1/4\).
