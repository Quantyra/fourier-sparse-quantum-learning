# Source and contribution assessment

Checked November 10, 2026. This is a bounded primary-source comparison, not an exhaustive novelty or priority certificate. No source-author outreach was performed. Standard Fourier identities are derived in the manuscript; imported results and their required access are identified below.

| Source/version | Named transfer question | What is used and what remains different |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Ye, arXiv:2610.17648v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17648v1), October 13, 2026 | Does the near-linear-copy construction also supply fixed-polynomial decoding? | Its explicit grouped dictionary and recovery establish a smaller sample bound. The inverse-weight averages in its coverage proof use unknown coefficients. Here estimated weights define a computable hypothesis and search pays the decoding cost. We do not claim that Ye's sample theorem is new. |
| [Arunachalam et al., corrected v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00481v4), November 10, 2021 | What earlier exact-learning and lower-bound benchmark is appropriate? | Retain the corrected version's O(k^(3/2) log^2 k) upper bound and its stated Omega(k log k) lower-bound regime. The two bounds are not called matching. |
| [Caro et al., v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.04843v2), December 7, 2023; ITCS 2024 | Is efficient exact learning already known? | Yes: Corollary 10 gives O(k^4 log(k^2/delta)) copies and polynomial computation. Corollary 3 is an approximate agnostic statement. Neither is the near-linear-copy joint guarantee proved here. |
| [Gopalan et al., author manuscript](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~rocco/Public/4-27-2011-gossw.pdf), April 27, 2011 | Can a positive missing coefficient be arbitrarily small? | Theorem 8.1 gives Boolean granularity and hence the weaker minimum 1/k used here. Its membership-query learning application already recovers sparse spectra efficiently with that stronger access. Our target does not provide membership queries. |
| [Kushilevitz--Mansour, journal record](https://doi.org/10.1137/0222080), 1993 | Where does chosen-input access needed by Fourier search come from? | It is available for the stored deterministic H. The target f is never queried. The exact bounded, capped prefix procedure used here is proved in full; the inspected journal abstract supplies attribution, not an uninspected black-box guarantee. |
| [Tropp, arXiv:1004.4389v7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1004.4389v7), June 15, 2011 | Can one concentration event cover the archive selected using its own fit batch? | Corollary 5.2 controls the fixed true-support Gram matrix. Every selected true subset is covered by its submatrices. Scalar Theorem 6.1(ii) bounds fresh residual correlations. Both applications specify their random variables and norms. |

Focused search phrases included “Fourier-sparse quantum polynomial time near-linear”, “Fourier-sparse quantum majority coefficients”, and “Fourier-sparse quantum examples polynomial exact learning 2026”. The check found no primary theorem with the same joint guarantee, but an absent hit does not establish novelty. Repeated mirror/search-result dates were not used as publication dates. The first attempted Caro HTML version was unavailable; its actual v2 PDF was inspected instead.

The manuscript's contribution is an explicit residual-relative hypothesis construction and complete near-linear-copy, polynomial-computation trajectory. It is not the first efficient quantum learner, the first near-linear quantum sample bound, a new Fourier-search principle or a new granularity theorem. The loose arithmetic exponents do not support a practical-performance claim. Independent validation, formal certification, noise robustness and the optimal logarithmic copy bound remain outside the result.
