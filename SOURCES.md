## Named checks prompted by Claude's review

Rechecked November 10, 2026 against the primary versions above. Ye's Theorem 1.1 states the sample guarantee, not a fixed polynomial computation bound. Algorithms 1--2 explicitly construct the candidate dictionary and solve over its coordinates. Section 5 uses the size bound `(k+1)^g`, with `g=O(log k/log log k)`. That estimate does not establish polynomial computation; it is not a lower bound on actual dictionary size or on all possible implementations. The manuscript now makes this distinction explicit.

GOSSW's April 27, 2011 author manuscript, printed page 21, Theorem 8.1, has the stated floor-log granularity minus one; the minimum-coefficient consequence is correct. Tropp v7, printed pages 20--21, Corollary 5.2, is the required matrix Chernoff bound. Printed pages 23--24, Theorem 6.1(ii), is the bounded-case Bernstein fraction with denominator `sigma^2+Rt/3`; part (iii), not (ii), is the split-regime form. The original pinpoints are retained, with additional arithmetic in the proof. These direct checks answer citation and interface questions; the model reviews themselves did not independently fetch those sources.

The same primary Tropp corollary was rechecked for Grok's exact-constant concern. For the rank-one PSD summands, dimension and norm bound are p and the expected sum is q I. The manuscript now gives the two corollary factors and derives the lower delta^2/2 and upper delta^2/3 exponents by scalar integration. This confirms the existing leading factor 300 at delta=1/10 without changing any budget.

## Version 0.3.0 computation refinement

The existing granularity, concentration, sample/computation comparison and prefix-search attribution remain applicable. Sharing samples across frozen same-depth tests uses the same explicitly derived bucket identity; correlated tests are handled by conditional concentration and a union bound. No new Fourier-search principle, sample improvement, practical-runtime or exhaustive novelty claim is made.

The exact fitting implementation uses the integer-preserving method of [Bareiss, Mathematics of Computation 22 (1968), 565-578](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1968-0226829-0). The transfer question is whether exact original-label fitting has polynomially bounded intermediate integers on unsuccessful tapes. Integer matrices and bordered-minor identities provide that structure; the manuscript derives the required identity, minor sizes, exact divisions and conservative Cramer cost. The focused primary-index check confirmed attribution and the determinant interface; direct AMS/OSTI PDF retrieval failed, so no fresh full-paper validation is claimed.
